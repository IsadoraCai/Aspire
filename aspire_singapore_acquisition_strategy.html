01 · First-principles definition

Reach before the account choice; measure need afterwards.

A company can be worth reaching before it has opened an account. Future account opening and first transaction are validation outcomes, not eligibility rules for the audience.

Primary cohort · working definition

Newly incorporated Singapore Pte Ltds with operating intent

Use the company’s declared industry at registration as a preliminary signal. Exclude Investment holding from the primary cohort proxy, while keeping holding companies, Funds and SPVs visible as a separate secondary cohort. “Non-holding industry” does not prove active trading or Aspire eligibility.

Decision window
Reach as soon as incorporation is visible.

ACRA lists a corporate bank account among the post-registration next steps. Observe account opening within 30 days as the primary demand signal; use 90 days to inspect the long tail. The synthetic sample’s day-15 median is descriptive only, not a proven optimal trigger.

04 · Scope and exclusions

Keep the core narrow; keep adjacent cohorts visible.

Primary · estimated 50,500/year

New local Pte Ltds with an operating-business signal

Reach all firms outside the Investment holding industry while the early account decision is open. Use paid-up capital ≥S$1,001 and cross-border-fit industries to prioritize service intensity, not to exclude other otherwise relevant businesses.

Secondary · separate learning track

Investment holding, Funds and SPVs

Do not erase them from the addressable universe: Aspire publicly says it serves Funds and SPVs. Keep them separate because the synthetic sample shows lower early activity for Investment holding, while that pattern is not market evidence.

Excluded from the primary estimate
  • Sole proprietorships and LLPs: outside the chosen incorporated-Pte-Ltd segment and not in the official “Companies” denominator.
  • Foreign-company branches: not locally incorporated companies; a branch is an extension of its overseas parent.
  • Investment holding: removed only from the primary operating-intent proxy and retained as a secondary cohort.
Unquantified downward / fit adjustments
  • Inactive, dormant, shell or never-trading companies within non-holding industries.
  • Companies that do not meet Aspire onboarding, risk, residency or document requirements.
  • Founders who already chose another provider before a reachable touchpoint.
  • Sales already made through unobserved channels or companies without a reachable consented touchpoint.

These make the serviceable and winnable pools smaller than 50,500, but public data and the synthetic workbook cannot support an honest conversion or eligibility haircut. The funnel ends at an explicit unknown instead of inventing Aspire share.

Priorities

Three routes to test first

01 · CSP handoff · strategic scale

Run after partner-volume and privacy gates

The synthetic proxy suggests a meaningful channel hypothesis, but partner names are fictional. Require real partners with ≥267 eligible companies/month combined to finish 3,200 within 12 months; target ≥535/month for a six-month accrual.

02 · Paid intent search · fast learn

Start this read while partner gates run

Learn query volume, click costs and account outcomes in weeks while CSP volume and privacy checks proceed. This parallel sequence pairs a fast channel read with the slower, higher-scale randomized test. Search informs paid-channel economics and messaging only; self-selected searchers cannot supply the untouched CSP control rate.

03 · Accountant partners · trusted extension

Recruit alongside the CSP route

Build on Aspire’s existing accountant programme and validate client volume with real firms. Expect a longer partner setup than search and a less immediate moment than the incorporation checklist.

Why kill bulk registry cold email?It infers intent from registration and risks poor fit, complaints and deliverability damage. The Spam Control Act applies to unsolicited commercial electronic messages sent in bulk and sets requirements including an unsubscribe facility and accurate sender/header information; publishing an address does not itself mean the recipient consented. The PDPA excludes defined business contact information from its data-protection provisions, but that is not a blanket permission to use every scraped list. Require legal review for any outbound list use; the generic bulk-scrape tactic is not worth testing.

Part 3 · One experiment

Test the CSP handoff before building the integration.

First-principles question: does putting a useful Aspire account option inside the CSP’s existing post-registration checklist cause more newly incorporated businesses to open an Aspire account within 30 days than the normal checklist does?

Step 1 · QuestionEstimate incremental liftChange only the Aspire module, not the whole onboarding flow.
Step 2 · Feasibility gate2–3 CSPs; ≥267 eligible/monthCombined supply supports 3,200 randomized companies within 12 months; ≥535/month supports about six.
Step 3 · MeasurementOne account outcomeApproved Aspire account opened within 30 days per assigned business.
Step 4 · DecisionFixed sample and stop rule3,200 total tests a +2pp effect; a +1pp effect needs about 10,602.
1 · What changes

One module in the normal CSP welcome checklist.

The CSP keeps its existing email, sender, timing and checklist. Treatment businesses see one new co-branded block and tracked application link. Control businesses receive the normal checklist without the Aspire block.

Control · business as usualExisting CSP registration-completion email/checklist; no new Aspire prompt.
→
Treatment · one additionSame message with a short “Set up business banking” Aspire module and tagged application link. No pricing claim, incentive or other content changes.
2 · Who sees it

Randomize eligible companies, not email opens.

Eligible test population
  • New local-incorporated Singapore Pte Ltd clients served by the participating CSP.
  • Declared industry is outside Investment holding, matching the primary prospecting proxy.
  • Company is not a known existing Aspire account or a known application already in progress at assignment.
  • The CSP sends the checklist as part of its normal service communication; the test adds an optional link and does not transfer non-applicant contact details to Aspire.
Assignment and comparison

Use 2–3 real CSPs and randomize at company level 1:1 within CSP and incorporation week. Keep the standard email identical except for the treatment module. Analyze by assigned group (intention to treat), even if a message is not opened; do not compare only clickers. Automated assignment and suppressed control content reduce contamination when founders or CSP staff share information.

Launch go/no-go gate: before randomization, partner operations, privacy counsel and Aspire’s DPO must approve the data flow, the communication classification, the company-level outcome match and the aggregate reporting agreement. The CSP keeps non-applicant contact data; only approved pseudonymous outcome matching/arm aggregates are used. If this cannot be approved or the partner cannot supply the required denominator, do not run or claim an account-opening lift test.

Cross-founder word-of-mouth and CSP staff forwarding can contaminate control. Log known forwards; if partner workflows cannot keep the treatment block assignment-specific, randomize by team/branch instead and recalculate sample size for clustering before launch.

3 · Single success metric

30-day new Aspire account-opening rate.

Primary metric · intention-to-treat
Opened Aspire accounts ÷ assigned eligible companies

Numerator: a new Aspire business account is approved and opened within 30 calendar days after the CSP checklist is sent. Denominator: all eligible companies assigned to that arm. Compare treatment minus control in percentage points. Do not use clicks, applications or email opens as additional success metrics.

Why 30 days?

It aligns with the agreed near-term decision window and gives the account process time to complete. ACRA says a company can open a corporate account immediately after registration. The workbook’s 30-day behavior is synthetic and is not used as the Aspire baseline.

4 · How many businesses?

Plan for roughly 3,200 randomized companies.

The sample-size estimate is a planning calculation, not a claim about Aspire’s current conversion rate.

Two-proportion test assumptions
Control account-opening rate (planning assumption)3%
Minimum effect to detect+2 percentage points
Treatment rate used for planning5%
Statistical threshold / power5% two-sided / 80%
Approximate sample size1,506 per arm → round to 1,600About 3,200 eligible businesses total, split equally.
Smaller effect sensitivity: detecting 3% versus 4% takes about 5,301 per arm (10,602 total), over three times the rounded +2pp design.
Baseline, throughput and feasibility

First calculate a 30-day approved/opened-account rate from Aspire’s historical eligible local-Pte cohorts, ideally for the same CSP sources. If that data is unavailable, keep 3% as an explicit prospective assumption and recalculate before assignment. Paid-search conversion is not a valid substitute: searchers self-select and do not represent the untreated CSP cohort.

For 3,200 businesses: combined eligible supply must average at least 267/month for 12-month enrollment or 535/month for six months. Screen two or three real partners against that documented volume before launch. The synthetic 20% largest-agent and 53% top-three bucket shares cannot establish actual throughput because the workbook says those provider names are fictional.

A +2pp lift from 3% is a 67% relative increase and may be optimistic for one email module. Keep +2pp only if it is the minimum commercially worthwhile lift. If +1pp matters, the 10,602 sample and partner supply must be funded; otherwise choose a longer horizon or a lower-cost pilot and label it feasibility-only, not proof of lift.

5 · Stop or scale

Wait for the fixed sample and each company’s 30-day window.

Decision rule

At 1,600 assigned businesses per arm, calculate the treatment-minus-control difference in the single primary metric with a two-sided 95% confidence interval.

Scale only if the lift is positive and the 95% interval excludes zero.This is evidence that the new checklist module increased 30-day Aspire account openings.
Otherwise stop the rollout.If the estimate is zero/negative or the interval includes zero, do not expand this handoff on the current evidence.
Guardrails against a misleading read
  • Do not stop early for a promising interim result; finish the prespecified sample and observation window.
  • Keep assignment, message delivery and UEN/account matching auditable by the CSP.
  • Report the sample size, missing outcome matches and confidence interval with the result.
  • The test estimates the effect for participating CSP clients; it does not estimate the whole Singapore market or prove Aspire eligibility for all new companies.
Experiment decision

Test the low-tech CSP checklist module first. It uses a moment supported by ACRA guidance and the strongest sample reach hypothesis, while avoiding a costly platform build before incremental account openings are demonstrated.

One thing the AI got wrong—and I caught

A polished number hid a category error.

Early AI draft

“About 40,100 high-intent companies a year”

The draft multiplied 58,077 official 2025 company formations by 506/732 from the workbook and presented the result as a high-intent annual target pool.

What I challenged

The numerator was hindsight from synthetic records

The 506 account openings were observed after incorporation in a synthetic sample. Future account opening cannot define who was worth reaching beforehand. Also, 58,077 is a broad Companies count, not a local-Pte-Ltd denominator. What I learned about working with AI

Structure the problem, then keep questioning the answer.

Insight → strategy → execution

Separate the market definition, channel choices, experiment and asset. Give each stage its own question and output so assumptions do not quietly carry forward as facts.

Make evidence visible

Ask for the denominator, source definition, arithmetic and exclusions beside every important number. A clear-looking estimate still needs a data-quality and scope check.

Challenge, don’t just prompt

Use follow-up questions to test whether the model is mixing ex-ante targeting with ex-post outcomes. AI can speed up the work, but it cannot decide which uncertainty is acceptable.
